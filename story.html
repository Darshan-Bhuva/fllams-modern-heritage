The Saree Story

A saree is mostly one plain field of colour. All the craft sits at the edge.

The cloth

FLLAMS begins with Indian saree cloth — handloom cottons and silk-cottons from traditions like Ilkal, Kanjeevaram, Chanderi, Chettinad, Maheshwari and Banarasi weaving. The body of the cloth is matte and calm. It is made to be worn, not to shine all over.

The border

The border is where the weaver puts the work: gold zari, temple motifs, stripes, a wide band of colour. In a FLLAMS piece the border is never spread thin across the whole garment. It is placed once, in one place — down the centre, across the hem, on one sleeve, or around a cuff. One placement per garment. That restraint is what keeps a rich cloth wearable on a man in London or New York.

The border, close up — concept mockup.

The pallu

The pallu is the showpiece end of the saree, worn over the shoulder — the most decorated part. On a FLLAMS product page, the pallu moment is the detail close-up: the cuff, the toggle, the brocade, shown large, last. It is the part you notice after you have already liked the shirt.

Saree to shirt

One saree makes only a few garments. The body, the border and the pallu are cut from different sections, so no two pieces from the same saree can be alike. That is why some FLLAMS pieces are 1 of 1: when it is gone, it is gone. Core pieces are re-cut from repeatable cloth while the fabric lasts.
